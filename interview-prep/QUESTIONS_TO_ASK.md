# Smart Questions for Each Interview Round

> **Principle:** Every question should demonstrate that you researched the company, understand the role, and think strategically. Never ask a question Google could answer. Never ask about compensation, PTO, or timeline in technical/peer/leadership rounds.

---

## For the Hiring Manager

These questions show you've thought about the territory, the team, and how you'd ramp.

### 1. "What does the current Datadog footprint look like in the Southcentral territory -- are we primarily expanding existing accounts, or is there significant greenfield opportunity in verticals like energy and defense?"

**Why this works:** Shows you understand the difference between land-and-expand vs. greenfield motions, and that you've identified the key verticals in the territory. The answer tells you whether you'll be displacing competitors or evangelizing Datadog.

### 2. "How is the SE-to-AE pairing structured? Am I aligned to a specific set of AEs, or do I float across the territory based on deal complexity and vertical expertise?"

**Why this works:** Reveals team structure and whether you'll be a generalist or a specialist. Also signals that you understand the SE-AE dynamic is the core operating unit.

### 3. "What does the ramp plan look like for a new SE in Key Accounts? I'm curious about the balance between product certification, shadowing existing deals, and getting your own accounts."

**Why this works:** Shows you're thinking about how to be effective quickly, not just how to survive onboarding. The word "own" signals you want accountability, not hand-holding.

### 4. "How do you measure SE success beyond closed-won revenue? I'm thinking about things like POC win rate, technical win rate independent of deal outcome, or customer satisfaction scores."

**Why this works:** Demonstrates that you understand SE success isn't just about whether the AE closes the deal. It separates the technical win (SE's responsibility) from the commercial win (AE's responsibility).

## For Peers (Peer Connect)

These questions should feel like a conversation between colleagues, not an interrogation. You're trying to understand what the day-to-day is actually like.

### 1. "What's the toughest competitive displacement you've done in this territory? I'm curious about what vendor you replaced, what the turning point was, and how long the cycle took."

**Why this works:** Peers love talking about their wins. The answer gives you real competitive intel -- which vendors are entrenched, what arguments worked, how long deals actually take (vs. what the HM says).

### 2. "Walk me through your demo process -- do you build custom demos for every prospect, or do you have a library of scenarios you adapt? How much of the demo is live product vs. pre-built environments?"

**Why this works:** This is the most practical question you can ask a peer SE. The answer directly shapes how you'd prepare. It also shows you've thought about the demo round specifically.

### 3. "What's one thing you wish you'd known before you started here that would have made your first 90 days easier?"

**Why this works:** Disarmingly simple. Peers give honest answers to this because it's not a loaded question. The answer is always useful -- might be about internal tooling, team dynamics, or customer expectations.

### 4. "How much of your time is spent on strategic/enterprise accounts versus supporting the mid-market or commercial teams when they hit technical depth?"

**Why this works:** Reveals whether Key Accounts SEs are truly dedicated to enterprise or get pulled into other segments. The answer tells you about workload, scope boundaries, and team dynamics.

## For Leadership

These questions demonstrate strategic thinking, market awareness, and that you follow the company beyond the product page.

### 1. "Datadog has made significant investments in AI -- Bits AI, LLM Observability, the AI Agents Console, the MCP Server for coding agents. How do you see the SE role evolving as customers shift from monitoring traditional applications to monitoring AI applications? Does the SE need to become an AI/ML practitioner?"

**Why this works:** Shows you've done deep product research (most candidates can't name Bits AI or the MCP Server). The question about the SE role evolving demonstrates you think about career trajectory strategically. It also lets the leader talk about Datadog's vision, which they enjoy.

### 2. "Datadog has historically grown through a strong PLG motion -- developers adopt the free tier, usage expands, then enterprise sales closes the deal. For Key Accounts in a territory like Southcentral where many buyers are traditional enterprises (energy, defense, healthcare), how does the PLG motion interact with top-down enterprise selling?"

**Why this works:** This is a sophisticated question about Datadog's go-to-market tension. PLG works great in tech-forward companies but less well in regulated industries with procurement processes. Asking this shows you understand Datadog's business model and the specific challenges of the Southcentral market.

### 3. "I've noticed Datadog has been expanding into security (Cloud SIEM, CSPM, ASM) and developer tools (CI Visibility, DORA Metrics). As the platform broadens, does the SE need to be deep across all products, or does the team develop vertical or product specializations?"

**Why this works:** Addresses the real challenge of platform breadth. 20+ products is a lot for one SE to know deeply. The answer tells you about specialization paths, overlay teams, and how the SE org scales knowledge.

### 4. "What does the SE career path look like at Datadog after Key Accounts? I'm thinking about trajectories like Principal SE, SE Manager, Solutions Architect, or moving into product -- what have you seen successful SEs do?"

**Why this works:** Shows long-term commitment without being presumptuous. The word "successful" invites the leader to describe what excellence looks like, which gives you a roadmap.

## Questions to AVOID

| Question | Why It's Bad |
|---|---|
| "What's the salary range / OTE?" | Never ask in a technical, peer, or leadership round. Comp discussion is for recruiter/HR only, and ideally after you've demonstrated value. |
| "How much PTO do I get?" | Signals you're already thinking about time off before you've started. Save for the offer stage. |
| "When will I hear back about next steps?" | Makes you seem anxious. The recruiter will tell you. If you must, ask the recruiter after the round, not the interviewer. |
| "Can you describe the company culture?" | Too generic. Everyone says "collaborative, fast-paced, innovative." You learn nothing. Ask about specific team dynamics instead. |
| "What does Datadog do?" | Signals zero preparation. You should already know this cold. |
| "How many products does Datadog have?" | Same problem -- this is on the website. |
| "Is remote work available?" | This is a territory SE role. The answer is hybrid/travel by definition. Asking this suggests you didn't read the JD. |
| "What's your market share?" | Awkward for the interviewer. Public data exists. If you want competitive positioning info, ask the peer SE in the form of "who do we run into most in this territory?" |

## General Tips for Q&A Sections

1. **Ask a maximum of 2 questions per round.** Even if you have 4 prepared, pick the 2 most relevant based on how the conversation went. Asking too many feels like an interrogation.

2. **Listen to the answer.** The worst thing you can do is ask a great question, then zone out while they answer because you're thinking about your next question. React to their answer. Ask a follow-up if it's interesting.

3. **Take notes visibly.** When they answer your question, write something down. It signals that you value their input. Even if you have a perfect memory, the act of note-taking is a social signal of respect.

4. **If your prepared question was already answered during the conversation, say so.** "I was going to ask about X, but you actually covered that when you talked about Y. What I'm curious about now is Z." This shows you were paying attention.

5. **End with gratitude, not another question.** Your last words should be something like "Thank you -- this was really helpful. I'm excited about the territory and the role." Not a final rapid-fire question.
